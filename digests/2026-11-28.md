## 1. PACE: Policy Augmentation and Compensation With Explicit Disturbance Estimation for Robust Locomotion · 4.42
Shixuan Han, Yuyang Wang, Jingting Jia, Zeshuai Chen, Jindou Jia, Kexin Guo, Xiang Yu (Beihang University)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27061 · code: none found
Tag: **Must-read** · Pick: taste · Evidence: real robot (DeepRobotics Lite3: 11.5 kg payload, 16 cm step-downs, 5 outdoor terrains)
Why read: Instead of hedging with domain randomization, it lumps payload, CoM shift, actuator degradation and pushes
into one joint-space disturbance, recovered online by a nonlinear observer (no joint accelerations, no force sensors).
The estimate feeds both a feedforward torque term and the policy observation. Trained on flat ground with ≤3 kg added mass,
it carried a payload about equal to the robot's own mass on hardware. A proprioceptive-estimation idea you can drop into a stairs/slopes policy.

## 2. Riding the Wave: Sim-to-Real Humanoid Control on Elastic Terrain (RIPPLE) · 4.18
Yinglei Zhu, Qin Li, Honghao Pan, Bozhao Wang, Binghui Xu, Jie Lu, Wentao Song, Xiyu Wang, Xinyu Zhu, Wei Liu, Xinjun Liu, Kai Lu, Huichan Zhao (Tsinghua, MagicLab et al.)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34569 · project: https://fly-pigTH.github.io/wave_rider/ (code "soon")
Tag: **Must-read** · Pick: taste · Evidence: real robot (MagicBot Z1 humanoid, zero-shot on a trampoline)
Why read: Adds an ElasticMembrane (pretensioned mass-spring mesh with deflection, energy storage and rebound) to mjlab for
GPU-parallel training, then a staged curriculum carries rigid-ground skills to balancing and consecutive jumping on deformable
terrain from proprioception only. One policy tolerates 0.35–2.8× the training stiffness, so no calibration is needed.
Directly on your mjlab + proprioceptive terrain-robustness track, and a good example of a curriculum design.

## 3. AirLeg: A Robust and Accurate Self-Supervised Visual-Inertial-Leg SLAM System · 4.01
Kuan Xu, Tianxin Hu, Ruofei Bai, Ruimeng Liu, Yuefan Hao, Zeyu Jiang, Zitong Zhan, Shenghai Yuan, Chen Wang, Lihua Xie (NTU, A*STAR et al.)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32523 · code: none found
Tag: **Skim** · Pick: taste · Evidence: real data (all 17 GrandTour sequences) + real low-cost quadruped on an embedded computer
Why read: The leg-odometry network is pre-trained in sim and then adapted without ground truth: bundle adjustment uses
leg odometry to refine the visual map, which in turn supervises the network, and it keeps adapting online. A deformation-aware
constraint ties loaded feet to the reconstructed terrain. Lowest error of 19 methods on GrandTour, 0.6% drift after 0.5 h of
unlabeled data. The same estimation theme as SBEE / SG-ODOM, now with a camera.

## 4. DynaMimic: A Physics-Informed World Model for Humanoid Motion Tracking · 3.70
Chaoyue Xing, Linyi Huang, Yiting He, Yixuan Yin, Siqi Hou, Yi Zhang, Liyuan Pan, Miaomiao Liu, Daqi Liu (ANU, AgiBot, BIT)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32944 · code: none found
Tag: **Must-read** · Pick: taste · Evidence: sim (LAFAN1, terrain clips) + real robot (AgiBot A3 humanoid)
Why read: A SuperMimic follow-up. Instead of an implicit next-state predictor, the world model predicts a contact wrench per
candidate support link plus a residual force and steps closed-form Lagrangian dynamics, which cuts error accumulation at support
changes. MPJPE drops 18.6% vs SuperMimic, and 11–15% on terrain clips with local height maps. On hardware: floor recovery,
a cartwheel and a box vault. SuperMimic family + height maps + fall recovery in one paper.

## 5. PushMimic: Learning Adaptive Humanoid Box Pushing via Dynamic Motion Augmentation and Reference Distillation · 3.37
Yulun Zhuang, Hanyu Zhang, Jingyu Liu, Yanran Ding (University of Michigan)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30077 · project: https://silvery107.github.io/pushmimic/
Tag: **Skim** · Pick: taste · Evidence: real robot (Unitree G1, inclined treadmill up to 12°, boxes up to 19 kg)
Why read: From one level-ground reference motion, sampling-based trajectory optimization builds a dynamically feasible dataset
over box mass and ramp slope, then distills it into one tracking policy that adapts torso pitch without observing those properties.
Success goes from 57.8% to 88.8% in sim. Relevant as a recipe for making motion-imitation policies work on slopes.

## 6. Trust What Repeats: Capped World-Model Policy Gradients for Humanoid Motion Tracking · 2.85
Xuzhe Dang, Stefan Edelkamp (Prague: Charles University et al.)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27648 · code: none found
Tag: **Skim** · Pick: taste · Evidence: sim (Unitree G1, 223 h of motion, 353 held-out motions)
Why read: An audit of SuperMimic's first-order gradients. 0.5% of imagined rollouts carry 22% of the gradient norm, and the
simulator confirms almost none of the largest ones. Capping each rollout's action-gradient at 10× the batch median, plus smarter
imagination starts, halves persistent falls on unseen clips and reaches the baseline return in half the time. A short, practical read if you try SuperMimic.

## 7. Embo-Harness: Towards Understanding Frontier-Model-as-Policy Harness · field
Xiao-Ming Wu, Kang Liao, … Zhongang Cai, Zhi Hou, Chen Change Loy (NTU S-Lab et al.)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31201 · project: https://dravenalg.github.io/projects/Embo-Harness/ · code: https://github.com/DravenALG/Embo-Harness
Tag: **FYI** · Pick: field · Evidence: sim (RoboLab-120) + real robot (4 manipulation tasks, 15/20 zero-shot) · HF: 106 upvotes
Why read: A systematic study of using a frontier multimodal model directly as the policy: prompting, planning, action execution and
tool use, distilled into six findings. Native visual reasoning and tool use beat handcrafted plans and skills, and spatial target
estimation matters more than low-level trajectory generation. 83.3% on RoboLab-120 (+26 pp). The week's biggest manipulation paper.

## 8. Motion Mamba 2.0: Autoregressive Diffusion Mamba for Long-Horizon Humanoid Control · field
Yue Huang, Tianshan Zhang, Zeyu Zhang, Jayeon Kang, Akide Liu, Sungjoon Choi, Dacheng Tao, Hao Tang (NTU, CASIA et al.)
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29897 · project: https://aigeeksgroup.github.io/MotionMamba2.0 · code: https://github.com/AIGeeksGroup/MotionMamba2.0
Tag: **FYI** · Pick: field · Evidence: real robot (Unitree G1: text-guided composition, keyboard/mouse locomotion) · HF: 228 upvotes (top of the week)
Why read: A Mamba-based diffusion motion generator with persistent content memory that stays controllable over ~40 s rollouts,
with a 6× faster CUDA-graph execution path. It is a kinematic generator feeding a tracker on the G1, so it's adjacent to your
motion-prior interest rather than core RL. Included for its outside signal.
